An intra-group licence of the LabourMarket.ai platform intellectual property
(software, source code, related know-how, and associated brand assets as

## 4. Functional analysis (functions, assets, risks)

### 4.1 Labour Market AI Sp. z o.o. (Licensor, PL)

- **Functions:** holds legal ownership of the platform IP; grants the licence;
  invoices the licence fee; participates in the 12-month fee review.
- **Assets:** the platform IP (software/code, know-how, brand as defined in
  the agreement). No operating platform infrastructure of its own for
  commercial exploitation.
- **Risks:** IP ownership risk (obsolescence, infringement claims against the
  IP), counterparty/credit risk on the licence fee, and — under the
  profit-contingent formula — the risk of receiving **zero** fee in
  loss-making months.
- **What it does NOT do:** does not sell to platform customers, does not
  operate the service, does not process user personal data in routine
  operation, does not employ platform operating staff.

### 4.2 UAB „Nonstop Group“ (Licensee, LT)

- **Functions:** operates the platform end to end; contracts with customers;
  issues customer invoices and collects payment; runs marketing, customer
  support and operator-coordinated matching; bears day-to-day operating
  decisions; acts as primary data controller under GDPR.
- **Assets:** operating infrastructure contracts (hosting etc.), customer
  contracts and relationships, workforce/contractor arrangements used to run
  the platform.
- **Risks:** market risk (demand), customer credit risk, operating cost risk,
  regulatory/compliance risk of operating the marketplace, data-protection
  risk as controller.

## 6. Chosen fee model and why (year 1)

**Fee model (first 12 calendar months from [AGREEMENT EFFECTIVE DATE]):**
Monthly Licence Fee = **30% × Positive Adjusted Net Profit** from
LabourMarket.ai activity of the previous calendar month; zero or negative
Adjusted Net Profit → fee = 0 EUR for that month. Adjusted Net Profit is
computed BEFORE the licence fee itself (no circularity), before corporate
income tax, and excludes dividends, non-platform financing costs, shareholder
personal expenses, and unjustified one-offs. Statement by the 10th business
day of the following month; Licensor invoices per statement; payment within 14
calendar days; currency EUR; errors corrected in the next month's statement.
A 12-month review starts at least 30 days before the period ends; changes
require a written amendment; absent a signed amendment the existing formula
continues.

**Why a profit-contingent royalty was chosen for year 1:**

1. **Startup phase.** The platform is at a very early commercial stage; there
   is no stable revenue history on which to base a fixed royalty.
2. **No reliable comparables identified yet.** No benchmarking study exists
   (see §7); setting a fixed rate now would be arbitrary in both directions.
3. **Alignment with actual profitability.** The licensor is paid only when the
   licensed IP actually generates profit for the operator, which reflects the
   economic reality that the IP's value is unproven.
4. **Avoids fixed-fee cash strain.** A fixed fee could push the early-stage
   operator into losses and create artificial intercompany debt.

**Economic rationale of the 30% rate:** the parties' commercial intent is that
the operator retains the larger share (70%) of platform profit, reflecting
that the operator performs the routine and entrepreneurial operating
functions, bears the market and operating risks, and performs
maintenance/exploitation DEMPE functions, while the licensor's contribution is
the licensed IP. **The 30% figure is a starting commercial agreement between
related parties. It has NOT been benchmarked and is NOT asserted to be
arm's-length. It is expressly subject to the TP analysis and the contractual
12-month review.**

## 9. Candidate TP methods (for the adviser to evaluate and select)

The following methods should be evaluated. **The appointed tax adviser — not
this memo — selects the method and produces the supporting analysis.**

- **CUP (Comparable Uncontrolled Price):** would require comparable licence
  agreements (internal or database-derived). None identified yet.
- **TNMM (Transactional Net Margin Method):** could test the operator's net
  margin as the tested party (routine operator return, residual to the IP) —
  requires a benchmarking set.
- **Profit split:** may be relevant given the DEMPE picture (both parties
  contribute to the value chain); the current 30/70 contingent split is
  structurally closest to a profit-split outcome, but the split factor must be
  supported by contribution analysis, not asserted.

## 10. Documentation thresholds — Lithuania and Poland

Both Lithuania and Poland impose transfer-pricing documentation obligations
(local file and, above certain sizes, master file) when statutory transaction
value and/or taxpayer size thresholds are met, and Poland additionally has TP
reporting/formalities for related-party transactions. **This memo deliberately
does not state any threshold amounts.** The applicable Lithuanian and Polish
thresholds, deadlines, and forms **must be checked against current law by the
tax adviser** and confirmed in writing before the first invoice, and
re-checked annually.
